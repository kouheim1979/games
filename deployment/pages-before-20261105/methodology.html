Follow the evidence, inspect the assumptions and see what remains unverified.

By Everyday Japan Lab · Reviewed 2026-10-10

Start with a question you can act on

Each guide should help with a concrete task: measuring a toilet, comparing a water-heating scenario, counting emergency supplies or following a bounded rice-cooking method. We explain what information you need, what the result means and which questions still need a manufacturer, local authority or qualified professional.

Check the source and its limits

  1. Find the original document. Prefer a public agency, standards body or the manufacturer responsible for the specification. Link the document so that you can inspect it.
  2. Identify what it actually measures. Record the date, country, product model, units and test conditions where supplied. Shipments are not installed households; a recommendation is not a measure of adoption.
  3. Keep the claim within that scope. A fit chart for one model is not a universal size rule. A recipe for a named pan and batch is not evidence that it works in every pot.
  4. Show the remaining uncertainty. Label an example, assumption or manufacturer claim rather than presenting it as a measured result from this site.

The source register lists the scope and check date for each reference. A check date records a review of that source; it is not the source’s publication date or a guarantee that it has not changed. Use the current manual and local requirements before making a purchase or installation decision.

Separate desk research from hands-on testing

The current guides are desk research. We have not physically tested or certified the linked products, and the rice guide has not been kitchen-tested by Everyday Japan Lab. Original diagrams illustrate a concept or a measurement location; they are not installation drawings. A worked example shows arithmetic using stated assumptions, not observed household performance.

AI assistance is used in drafting and code development. It does not replace a primary source or turn an unverified claim into evidence. The publisher remains responsible for the published material.

Make the tools inspectable

Tool pages show their units, formulas, example inputs and exclusions. You can reproduce a worked example and change one input at a time. Input limits catch invalid entries; they do not establish that an installation is safe or that a household’s supplies are sufficient. A negative saving is a possible result, not a reason to hide the comparison.

Software checks can verify a calculation against an expected result. They cannot establish product fit, food quality, real-world energy use or emergency readiness. Read each guide’s stated limits alongside its result.

Report a problem with enough detail to check it

Use Contact and corrections to send the page URL, the sentence or calculation in question and a primary source if you have one. For a tool issue, include non-sensitive example inputs and the expected and actual result. Do not send passwords, payment details or private health information.

Corrections should change the affected explanation or formula and its reviewed date. A reader can then compare the revised claim with its linked source. The contact page is the current reporting route; this site does not provide individual installation approval or emergency assistance.
